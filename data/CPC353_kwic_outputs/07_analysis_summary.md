# English-Malay Code-Switching KWIC Analysis

## Scope and annotation assumptions

The analysis accepts the team's manual labels as the source of truth. Switching types are multi-label, so one review may contribute to more than one type and percentages do not sum to 100%. Proper nouns, brand names, restaurant names, and specific dish names were excluded during team annotation where possible; general food words were treated as ordinary lexical items.

## Interpretation guidance

A keyword occurrence is not automatically a code-switching event. Interpret each concordance line using its left and right context. Record whether it is an intra-sentential switch, inter-sentential switch, tag switch, general food term, proper name, borrowing, monolingual use, or uncertain case.

## Research-question answer

The preliminary review-level result indicates that intra-sentential switching is the dominant English-Malay code-switching pattern in this food-review corpus. The final discussion should support this distribution with selected KWIC examples and explain where the language boundary occurs.
